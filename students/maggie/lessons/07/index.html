Say more, with a clear purpose

You want to contribute more than a short yes or a list of actions in meetings. Add one useful detail: the reason, the owner, the deadline or the help you need.

Start here about 10 minutes

Choose one real task. Say what needs doing, who will do it and by when.

Then add one reason. You do not need a longer sentence—or a more complicated word—for every idea.

Patterns from your speaking

Lightly reconstructed from your conversation, not exact quotations. Try one correction out loud before opening it.

ProgressIn Lesson 04 you said, “I also have a meeting with customers last Friday.” This time you said, “Last Friday I had a face-to-face meeting with our customer.” The past verb and the singular noun are both right. One small thing left: we talked about a lot of topics.

Check 1

Numbers and articles

  • Usually when we have five person in the meeting, I just talk five sentence.

    Say it naturally

    Usually, when there are five people in the meeting, I only say about five sentences.

    After a number, use the plural: five people, five sentences. For something that happens regularly, use the present.

  • This time I could speak one more minutes or two more minutes.

    Say it naturally

    Next time, I'll try to speak for one or two more minutes.

    One takes a singular noun (one minute). Two or more takes -s. Use next time for a plan.

  • I couldn't remember all the words or sentence.

    Say it naturally

    I can't remember all the words and sentences.

    After all, use plural nouns. Use can't to describe your situation now.

  • Let me give a example.

    Say it naturally

    Let me give an example.

    Use an before a vowel sound: an example, an idea, an opposite opinion.

  • Holding a opposite opinion means I should speak more about my idea and the reason.

    Say it naturally

    Holding an opposite opinion means I should explain my idea and my reason.

    An opposite opinion, because opposite starts with a vowel sound. The everyday verb is disagree: I disagree because…

Check 2

Verbs and small words

  • Please wait me one minute.

    Say it naturally

    Please wait for me. / Please give me one minute.

    Wait for + person. Give me a minute is a polite way to ask for time.

  • I hope I could engage more into the discussion.

    Say it naturally

    I hope I can engage more in the discussion. / I hope I can contribute more to the discussion.

    Keep your word and fix the small word: engage in, contribute to. After hope, can describes something you want to be able to do.

  • I should focus on about how to speak more.

    Say it naturally

    I should focus on how to speak more.

    Use one preposition only: focus on + noun, or focus on how to…

  • Let me brief the tasks we need to be done by this week.

    Say it naturally

    Let me brief you on the tasks that need to be done this week.

    Brief someone on something. After tasks, use that need to be done. This week doesn't need by.

  • If you have any questions, please raise out now.

    Say it naturally

    If you have any questions, please raise them now. / Please bring them up now.

    Raise a question, without out. Bring up is a different expression with a similar meaning.

Later, one at a time

Don't try to fix everything at once. Pick one a week.

  • I always be afraid to speak some wrong words.

    Say it naturally

    I'm always afraid of saying the wrong words.

    Afraid needs be: I'm afraid. After afraid of, use -ing. We say words and phrases; we speak a language.

  • I always to keep myself very safe.

    Say it naturally

    I always try to play it safe.

    Your sentence needs a verb after always: try. Play it safe means avoid risks.

  • Do I have authority to highlight some words?

    Say it naturally

    Am I allowed to highlight some words? / Can I highlight some words?

    Authority means official power. For permission, use Am I allowed to…? or Can I…?

  • I already taking a project last week.

    Say it naturally

    I took on a project last week. / I'm already working on a project.

    Last week is finished past time, so use took. To describe the situation now, use am + -ing.

  • If this new task could move to next month, I think I will help at that time.

    Say it naturally

    If this task can be moved to next month, I can help then.

    After if, use the present (can be moved). Then is natural for a future time.

  • This is a recurring issues which happens on every weekend.

    Say it naturally

    This is a recurring issue that happens every weekend.

    One issue; several issues. Say every weekend without on. You can also say: This issue recurs every weekend.

  • I can familiar with it.

    Say it naturally

    I can become familiar with it.

    Familiar is an adjective, so can needs a verb: can become or can get familiar. Be familiar describes the state; become familiar describes the change.

Build a useful briefing

Your idea—align on the timeline and make responsibilities clear—already worked. These versions give you choices, not a single correct answer.

  1. What needs doing?Review the requests for the next sprint.
  2. Who will do it?Each team checks its own list.
  3. By when?Send the reviewed list by Friday.

Your idea, made smoother

We need to align on the timeline by this Friday. Everyone should know what they are responsible for. Please raise any questions now.

Listen, then say it with your own deadline.

Add a concrete task

The task at hand is to review the requests for the next sprint. Each team should check its own list and send it by Friday, so that we have time to prepare.

Practice scenario, not a new instruction for your workplace. The extra detail makes the action clear; the longer wording is not automatically better.

Start with the purpose
The purpose of this meeting is to agree on the next steps.
Start with the benefit
Planning ahead gives us time to review the requests.
Start with the situation
Given our current workload, we need to prioritise urgent requests.

Six things you can always add

When you want to say more than the action, choose one of these. You do not need all six.

The challenge

The main challenge is the short deadline.

What is difficult?

A way to solve it

One way to solve it is to review the list earlier.

What could help?

What you are already doing

We are already working on the first part.

What has started?

What is within your capacity

We can handle two requests this week.

What can you do?

What your team can provide

Our team can provide the data by Wednesday.

What can you offer?

Help you need

We need some help from another team with the review.

What do you need from others?

Before you add moreAsk yourself: Do these people already know this? If yes, summarise it in a sentence or two. If not, add the background. Also check the time: how many minutes do we have?

Clarity before formality“We need to agree on the timeline” is just as useful as “We need to align on the timeline.” Starting with I or we is not a mistake. Add context when the listener needs it, not just to make the sentence longer.

Three ways to give a reason

Your example was a recurring issue within your team's project scope. Here are three ways to express the same reason.

because
We need to resolve this issue because it is within our team's scope.A direct way to explain why. Because is entirely natural at work.
as
As this issue is within our team's scope, we need to resolve it.The reason comes first as background for the action.
since
Since this issue is within our team's scope, we need to resolve it.Here since gives a reason, not a starting time.

All three work in this context. As and since often present the reason as background. If the meaning could be confused with time, because is clearer. You do not need to replace every because.

Given = considering

Given our current workload, we need more time.

Given + a noun phrase means considering that situation. It is a different word from giving: “I'm giving the team an update.”

Reason, purpose or example?

because
why: because the deadline is Friday
so that
purpose: so that the team can prepare
such as
examples: such as access problems

  1.     our current workload, we need to prioritise urgent requests.

  2. We need to fix this     it is within our team's scope.

  3. Please review the list early     we can prepare for the next sprint.

  4. We handle urgent requests,     system failures and access problems.

Say no, offer a possibility

You gave a clear reason and suggested next month. Keep that structure; make the verb forms easier to use.

I don't have the bandwidth to take on this task right now because I took on another project last week. If the deadline moves to next month, I can help then.

Listen for the pauses: limit / reason / possibility.

Use it in a different situation

The limit
I can't take this on this week.
The reason
I'm already working on another project.
A possibility
If we move the deadline, I can help.

Keep the meaning

Beyond my bandwidth means more than I can currently manage. Within our scope means included in our agreed work or responsibility. One is about capacity; the other is about what the work covers.

“I don't have time for this right now” is also natural. Bandwidth is a useful workplace option, not a required upgrade.

  1. We need to align     the timeline before Friday.

  2. I took     another project last week.

  3. This issue happens     weekend.

  4. I     already working on another project, so I can't take this on.

Words and sounds to keep

Learn the expression with its surrounding words, rather than memorising a replacement word on its own.

the task at hand
the task we are dealing with nowThe task at hand is to review the list.
raise a question / bring up a concern
mention something for discussionI'd like to raise a question about the deadline.
contribute to a discussion
add your ideas or informationI want to contribute more to our discussions.
become familiar with
learn something until you know it wellReviewing the notes helps me become familiar with these patterns.
counterproductive
having the opposite effect to the one intendedTrying to change everything at once can be counterproductive.
pragmatic
focused on what is practical in the situationA pragmatic first step is to add one useful detail in each meeting.

Pronunciation we revisited

These are practice targets from our discussion, not a score of your pronunciation. Listen, pause and repeat. Your browser's available voice may vary.

  • modernizing MOD-er-ny-zing

    You noticed n and l can mix. Keep the n in modern-izing distinct from l: say modern first, then add -izing.

  • adjective AJ-ik-tiv

    You asked whether the d is silent. The d and j join into one sound, /dʒ/, as in job. Say AJ-ik-tiv, not ad-JEK-tiv with a separate d. This works in both British and American English.

  • technology · scenarios

    Stress the second syllable: tek-NOL-uh-jee · suh-NAIR-ee-ohz. Keep the plural ending in scenarios.

  • real

    Keep the opening r and the final l. Aim for a clear word in the whole phrase, not an exaggerated extra syllable.
