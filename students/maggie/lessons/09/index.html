Rule · quiz

Ago, since, for and more

You asked whether to say five years ago or from five years ago. It is five years ago. From needs two points. Here are the time words you will use most at work.

ago · one point in the past

counts back from now

  • I started learning English five years ago.
  • The truck left two hours ago.

since · a start, until now

use have + past participle

  • I have worked in logistics since 2019.
  • We have used this system since March.

for · how long

no start or end date needed

  • I studied with him for about two years.
  • I have worked here for five years.

from … to · start and end

you give both points

  • We had lessons from January to March.
  • The office is open from 9 to 5.

by or until

deadline or continuing

  • Please send it by Friday. (no later than Friday)
  • I'm on leave until Monday. (up to Monday)

during or while

noun or full clause

  • During the promotion, volume doubled.
  • I checked email while I was waiting.

Since and forThey usually go with have when it is still true: I have worked here for five years. When it is finished, use the past: I worked there for two years.

Learn · quiz

Words that go together

Learn a word with its partners, not alone. Write responsible for, not just responsible. These are the partners you will meet most often at work.

adjective + word

the small word is part of it

  • I'm eager to learn.
  • Be aware of the risk.
  • She is responsible for planning.
  • I'm familiar with this system.

verb + word

learn the pair together

  • We deal with customers every day.
  • I rely on my supplier.
  • Please focus on one task.
  • Everyone should contribute to the project.

verb + noun at work

the usual verb for each noun

  • meet a deadline
  • make a decision
  • raise a concern
  • give feedback

fixed pieces

say them as one chunk

  • the same 24 hours
  • a wide range of options
  • a number of reasons
  • in terms of cost

3

Compare

Busy, or spread too thin?

You asked whether a very busy day means you spread yourself too thin. It does not, unless the work suffers.

A hectic day

Busy, but you handled it

Yesterday was hectic. I took my parents to the doctor and answered work messages in between.

Spread too thin

Too many things, none done well

I'm spread too thin. I want to improve my English, but I have too many other projects.

Similar phrasesI have a lot on my plate means I have a lot to deal with. I'm swamped is informal for too much work right now.

Tell the exam story in three steps

You knew all the words. Put one idea in each sentence and join them with so and because.

  1. What went wrongWhen I entered the exam room, the system failed to match my face with my passport photo.
  2. What happened nextSo the exam started late. That was a delay.
  3. How it feltIt was a setback for getting my certificate.

Small wordsRegister for an exam. Enroll in a course. I ran into some obstacles means I met some problems on the way.
