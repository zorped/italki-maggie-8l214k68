Scale at work

You asked how to use scale in your own work. Lesson 05 gave you the basic meanings. Here are the patterns you will actually need, with your servers and your customers.

scale up · scale down

make bigger · make smaller

  • We need to scale up the database as the customer grows.
  • After the promotion, we scale down the extra shifts.

scale back

reduce on purpose

  • We had to scale back our plans because of the budget.
high scalability: the noun. “Customers want high scalability” means they want a system that can grow without being rebuilt.

Your situation, said clearlyAs this customer's business grew, we had to scale up the database to support the extra data. The servers are scalable, so we can add more with very few changes.

Plan, strategy, roadmap, blueprint

All four mean a kind of plan. They are not the same plan. Ask what the plan is for.

plan
the general word: what you intend to doWe have a plan for the delivery on Friday.
strategy
the overall approach to reach a long-term goal, chosen from other optionsOur strategy is to win customers by delivering faster than anyone else.
roadmap
the steps over time, with milestones, from now to the goalThe roadmap has three phases and ends with full automation in 2027.
blueprint
a detailed design that shows exactly how something will be built or organisedThe blueprint shows where every shelf, door and loading bay will go.

A short way to rememberA strategy says what approach. A roadmap says in what order and when. A blueprint says exactly how it is built.

How skilled, how careful

Many words mean good at something or careful. At C1 you choose the exact one. Each line says what is different.

capable (of + -ing)
has the ability, even if it has not been shown yetShe is capable of leading the team.
skilled (at / in)
has learned the skill through training and practiceShe is a skilled welder.
proficient (in)
reaches a high, usually tested level in a defined skillHe is proficient in Excel.
adept (at)
quick and naturally good at something that needs judgmentShe is adept at handling difficult customers.
meticulous
extremely careful about very small detailsHe is meticulous about labelling every box.
diligent
describes a person who works carefully and steadily, with real effortShe is a diligent employee.
conscientious
describes a person who takes their responsibilities seriously and does the job properlyHe is a conscientious team member.
painstaking
describes work that takes great care, time and effortThe painstaking search took weeks.

Where competent sitsYou met competent, thorough and rigorous in Lesson 05. Competent means meets the standard. Capable promises less, because it only says the ability is there. Proficient and adept say more.

Competent or competitive?You said workers in internet companies have a competent salary. The word you want is competitive: pay that is high compared with other employers.

Sounds and spelling

These are the new words from this lesson. Practice targets, not a score. Listen, pause and repeat. Your browser's available voice may vary.

  • scalability skay-luh-BIL-uh-tee

    Stress BIL. Scalable is SKAY-luh-bul.

  • strategy STRAT-uh-jee

    Three syllables. The g sounds like j.

  • meticulous muh-TIK-yuh-lus

    Stress TIK, the second syllable. Four syllables in all.

  • proficient pruh-FISH-uhnt

    Stress FISH. The -cient ending sounds like -shuhnt.

  • competitive kuhm-PET-uh-tiv

    Stress PET. Compare competent, which is KOM-puh-tuhnt.

  • diligent DIL-uh-juhnt

    Stress DIL. The g sounds like j.

A speaking strategyIf a word is hard to say in the moment, use plain words you can say clearly. Instead of meticulous, say very careful about details. Say the hard word again once you have practiced it.

American or British spellingBoth are correct. Choose one and keep it the same through a document. analyze / analyse · center / centre · color / colour.

Speaking

Say it out loud

Say your own answer first. The hidden versions are models, not scripts you have to copy.

  1. 1

    Explain what happens to a customer's system when its business grows. Use scale up and scale down.

    Compare with an example answer

    When a customer's volume grows, we scale up its database and add servers. When the busy period is over, we scale down again. Scalable systems let us do that with very few changes.

  2. 2

    Describe a goal at work. Use strategy and roadmap.

    Compare with an example answer

    Our strategy is to keep our key customers by being reliable. The roadmap has two phases: first we move the data, then we add more servers.

  3. 3

    Describe a colleague with one word for skill and one for care.

    Compare with an example answer

    My colleague is adept at calming angry customers, and she is meticulous about recording every detail.

  4. 4

    Say how a plan changed because of a limit. Use scale back.

    Compare with an example answer

    Our budget was cut, so we had to scale back our plans for next year.
